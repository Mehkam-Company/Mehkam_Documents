# Decision-Making Framework

<div align="center">
  <img src="../../04-brand/assets/logo/mehkam-symbol.svg" alt="Mehkam Logo" width="150" />
</div>

## Overview

This document defines the official decision-making framework of Mehkam Holding.

The purpose of this framework is to establish how decisions are created, evaluated, approved, documented, executed, and reviewed across Mehkam Holding and its Business Units.

Mehkam follows a decentralized decision-making model in which Business Units maintain operational independence while Mehkam protects strategic alignment, ownership, governance, risk management, and long-term ecosystem value.

The framework establishes clear boundaries between:

- Operational decisions.
- Strategic decisions.
- Financial decisions.
- Organizational decisions.
- Ownership and legal decisions.

The objective is to ensure that decisions are made at the appropriate organizational level without creating unnecessary centralized bottlenecks.

# Purpose

The purpose of the Mehkam Decision-Making Framework is to establish a consistent system for decision authority, accountability, escalation, approval, documentation, and review.

This framework exists to:

- Define decision ownership.
- Establish decision-making authority.
- Protect Business Unit autonomy.
- Protect Mehkam's strategic interests.
- Establish escalation mechanisms.
- Ensure important decisions remain traceable.
- Reduce unnecessary organizational bottlenecks.
- Improve decision quality and accountability.

# 1. Decision-Making Philosophy

Mehkam believes decisions should be made by the teams closest to the problem.

Business Units own their execution, operations, and day-to-day business decisions.

Mehkam provides governance, strategic review, ownership protection, and approval for decisions that may materially affect the long-term direction, financial position, ownership structure, or ecosystem of Mehkam.

The core principle is:

> Business Units own execution. Mehkam protects strategic direction and long-term value.

Decision authority should remain as close to execution as possible while maintaining appropriate governance controls.

# 2. Decision Ownership Model

Every important decision should clearly define:

- Decision Owner.
- Decision Context.
- Problem or Opportunity.
- Available Options.
- Decision Rationale.
- Expected Outcome.
- Approval Requirements.
- Responsible Executor.
- Documentation Record.
- Review Requirements.

## 2.1 Business Unit Responsibilities

Business Units are responsible for:

- Identifying opportunities and problems.
- Evaluating available options.
- Preparing recommendations.
- Making decisions within their authority.
- Executing approved decisions.
- Measuring outcomes.
- Documenting important decisions.
- Escalating decisions that exceed their authority.

## 2.2 Mehkam Responsibilities

Mehkam is responsible for:

- Strategic alignment.
- Governance oversight.
- Ownership protection.
- Portfolio-level risk evaluation.
- Approval of governance-level decisions.
- Protection of long-term ecosystem value.
- Providing strategic guidance when required.

Mehkam should not take ownership of operational decisions unless the decision falls outside the Business Unit's authority or requires holding-level intervention.

# 3. Operational Decision Authority

Operational decisions are managed independently by Business Units.

Examples include:

- Engineering decisions.
- Technology selection.
- Product implementation.
- Internal workflows.
- Development processes.
- Operational procedures.
- Routine business execution.
- Team-level execution decisions.

These decisions do not require Mehkam approval when they remain within the Business Unit's approved authority and strategic boundaries.

Business Units remain accountable for:

- Execution quality.
- Operational performance.
- Resource efficiency.
- Business outcomes.

# 4. Strategic Decision Authority

Strategic decisions require Mehkam review and approval when they materially affect the long-term direction or strategic position of a Business Unit or the broader Mehkam ecosystem.

Examples include:

- Entering new markets.
- Major business model changes.
- Significant product direction changes.
- Long-term business initiatives.
- Major strategic partnerships.
- Material changes to business positioning.
- Decisions with significant portfolio-level impact.

The general decision flow is:

```text
Business Unit
        ↓
Strategic Proposal
        ↓
Mehkam Review & Approval
        ↓
Business Execution
```

Business Units remain responsible for implementation after approval.

# 5. Financial Decision Authority

Major financial decisions require Mehkam review and approval.

Examples include:

- Significant investments.
- Major budget expansions.
- Large resource allocations.
- Significant financial commitments.
- Material changes to financial strategy.
- Major capital expenditures.

The general decision flow is:

```text
Business Unit
        ↓
Financial Proposal
        ↓
Mehkam Financial Review
        ↓
Approval
        ↓
Execution
```

Routine financial activities performed within approved budgets and authority boundaries remain under Business Unit control.

# 6. Organizational Decision Authority

Major organizational decisions require Mehkam review and approval when they materially affect the structure, leadership, or strategic capabilities of a Business Unit.

Examples include:

- Leadership changes.
- Major team restructuring.
- Creation of new departments.
- Significant organizational redesign.
- Major changes to executive responsibilities.
- Organizational changes with significant financial or strategic impact.

The general flow is:

```text
Business Unit
        ↓
Organizational Proposal
        ↓
Mehkam Review
        ↓
Approval
        ↓
Implementation
```

Routine team management and operational staffing decisions remain under Business Unit authority.

# 7. Ownership and Legal Decision Authority

Ownership and legal decisions follow a hybrid authority model.

## 7.1 Mehkam Approval Required

The following decisions require Mehkam approval:

- Equity changes.
- Ownership structure changes.
- Company acquisition.
- Company sale.
- Transfer of material business assets.
- Major strategic legal commitments.
- Material changes to corporate structure.
- Decisions that materially affect Mehkam's ownership rights.

## 7.2 Business Unit Authority

Within approved boundaries, Business Units may manage:

- Standard operational contracts.
- Routine legal activities.
- Customer agreements.
- Supplier agreements.
- Business execution agreements.
- Other ordinary-course legal activities.

Business Units must remain within the authority and legal boundaries established by Mehkam.

# 8. Decision Documentation Process

Each Business Unit maintains its own decision documentation system.

Business Units must document important decisions in a way that allows the decision to be understood and reviewed later.

Important decision records should include:

- Decision title.
- Decision owner.
- Context.
- Problem or opportunity.
- Options considered.
- Decision rationale.
- Expected impact.
- Risks.
- Approval authority.
- Final decision.
- Outcome.

The general process is:

```text
Decision Evaluation
        ↓
Decision Documentation
        ↓
Internal Approval
        ↓
Execution
        ↓
Outcome Tracking
        ↓
Reporting to Mehkam
```

Decision documentation should prioritize clarity, traceability, and practical usefulness.

# 9. Decision Escalation Process

Business Units may escalate complex or high-impact decisions to Mehkam when strategic guidance, approval, or alignment is required.

Escalation does not automatically transfer operational ownership of the decision.

Business Units remain responsible for:

- Problem definition.
- Analysis.
- Available options.
- Recommendation.
- Execution.
- Outcome ownership.

The general escalation flow is:

```text
Business Unit
        ↓
Problem Identification
        ↓
Decision Analysis
        ↓
Proposal to Mehkam
        ↓
Guidance / Approval
        ↓
Execution
        ↓
Outcome Review
```

Escalation should be used to resolve authority boundaries and strategic uncertainty, not to transfer routine responsibility to the holding company.

# 10. Decision Review Process

Business Units report important decisions through periodic review processes with Mehkam.

Review objectives include:

- Maintaining transparency.
- Ensuring strategic alignment.
- Identifying emerging risks.
- Reviewing outcomes.
- Providing strategic guidance.
- Identifying opportunities for improvement.

The general review flow is:

```text
Business Unit
        ↓
Decision Report
        ↓
Review Meeting
        ↓
Mehkam Feedback
        ↓
Continuous Improvement
```

Decision reviews should focus on outcomes and organizational learning rather than unnecessary operational intervention.

# 11. Decision-Making Principles

## 11.1 Long-Term Value First

Decisions should prioritize sustainable long-term value over short-term optimization when the two objectives conflict.

## 11.2 Evidence Before Assumptions

Important decisions should be supported by relevant data, analysis, experiments, customer evidence, financial information, or other appropriate evidence.

## 11.3 Independent Execution

Teams closest to the problem should control execution whenever they possess the required authority and capability.

## 11.4 Strategic Alignment

Business Unit decisions must remain consistent with Mehkam's vision, strategy, governance requirements, and long-term objectives.

## 11.5 Documentation First

Important decisions must remain traceable so that their context, rationale, ownership, and outcomes can be understood over time.

## 11.6 Governance Without Bottlenecks

Governance should protect ownership, strategic alignment, and long-term value without unnecessarily slowing execution.

## 11.7 Clear Accountability

Decision authority and decision accountability should remain clearly defined.

The person or team making a decision should understand the expected outcome and remain accountable for execution within their authority.

# Scope

This framework applies to:

- Mehkam Holding.
- All Business Units.
- Subsidiaries.
- Leadership teams.
- Engineering teams.
- Business teams.
- Strategic initiatives.
- Portfolio-level decisions.

It applies to operational, strategic, financial, organizational, ownership, and legal decisions within the Mehkam ecosystem.

The framework should be applied together with Mehkam's governance, operating, investment, and strategic documents.

# Governance

The Mehkam Decision-Making Framework is an official organizational framework.

Decision authority should be reviewed whenever:

- A Business Unit is created.
- A Business Unit materially changes its scale.
- Organizational responsibilities change.
- Governance structures change.
- Ownership structures change.
- Major strategic initiatives are introduced.

Material changes to this framework must follow Mehkam's governance and documentation review process.

The framework should be periodically reviewed to ensure that governance remains strong without creating unnecessary centralized decision-making.

# Final Statement

The Mehkam Decision-Making Framework establishes a balance between Business Unit independence and holding-level governance.

Business Units maintain ownership of execution and operational decisions, while Mehkam protects strategic alignment, ownership, governance, and long-term ecosystem value.

The objective is to make decisions at the lowest appropriate organizational level while ensuring that decisions with material strategic, financial, organizational, ownership, or legal consequences receive the appropriate level of review.

Through clear authority, accountability, evidence-based decisions, and disciplined documentation, Mehkam aims to build an organization that can scale its decision-making capability without sacrificing execution speed or strategic control.
