# Risk Management Framework

<div align="center">
  <img src="../../04-brand/assets/logo/png/mehkam-symbol-white-2048w.png" alt="Mehkam Logo" width="150" />
</div>

## Overview

This document defines the official risk management framework of Mehkam Holding.

The purpose of this framework is to establish how risks are identified, evaluated, owned, mitigated, monitored, documented, and reported across Mehkam Holding and its independent Business Units.

Mehkam follows a decentralized risk management approach.

Business Units are responsible for managing the risks associated with their own operations, while Mehkam maintains governance oversight, strategic visibility, and protection of the long-term value of the ecosystem.

The core principle is:

> Business Units manage their risks. Mehkam ensures governance, visibility, and long-term protection.

The framework is designed to preserve Business Unit independence while ensuring that material risks remain visible at the appropriate organizational level.

# Purpose

The purpose of the Mehkam Risk Management Framework is to establish a consistent system for risk ownership, assessment, mitigation, monitoring, escalation, and governance.

This framework exists to:

- Protect Mehkam-owned businesses.
- Reduce unexpected business disruption.
- Improve organizational decision quality.
- Establish clear risk ownership.
- Increase organizational risk awareness.
- Support sustainable business growth.
- Protect long-term business and portfolio value.
- Provide Mehkam with appropriate visibility into material risks.

# 1. Risk Management Philosophy

Mehkam believes that risk ownership should remain as close as possible to the source of the risk.

Each Business Unit is responsible for identifying, evaluating, mitigating, and monitoring risks related to its own operations.

Business Units must develop internal risk awareness and maintain appropriate processes for managing risks within their authority.

Mehkam does not directly manage the daily operational risks of Business Units.

Mehkam is responsible for:

- Governance oversight.
- Strategic risk visibility.
- Portfolio-level risk awareness.
- Ecosystem protection.
- Long-term value preservation.

The general risk management model is:

```text id="q9wh2f"
Business Unit
        ↓
Risk Identification
        ↓
Risk Assessment
        ↓
Risk Mitigation
        ↓
Risk Monitoring
        ↓
Periodic Reporting to Mehkam
        ↓
Governance Review
```

# 2. Risk Management Objectives

The primary objectives of the Mehkam Risk Management Framework are to:

- Protect Mehkam-owned businesses.
- Reduce unexpected business disruption.
- Improve decision quality.
- Increase organizational risk awareness.
- Establish clear accountability.
- Support sustainable growth.
- Preserve long-term business value.
- Improve organizational resilience.
- Maintain appropriate governance visibility.

Risk management should support business execution rather than create unnecessary operational bureaucracy.

# 3. Risk Ownership Model

Every identified risk must have a clearly assigned Risk Owner.

The Risk Owner must belong to the Business Unit responsible for the relevant area of the business.

Risk ownership ensures:

- Clear accountability.
- Faster response.
- Better risk visibility.
- Continuous monitoring.
- Effective mitigation.

The general ownership model is:

```text id="p0xgqk"
Risk Identification
        ↓
Assign Risk Owner
        ↓
Risk Assessment
        ↓
Mitigation Plan
        ↓
Monitoring
        ↓
Reporting
```

## 3.1 Business Unit Responsibilities

Business Units are responsible for:

- Identifying risks within their domain.
- Assigning Risk Owners.
- Evaluating probability and impact.
- Creating mitigation plans.
- Executing mitigation actions.
- Monitoring risk status.
- Reviewing risk changes.
- Reporting significant risks.
- Maintaining appropriate risk documentation.

Business Units retain ownership of their operational risks.

## 3.2 Risk Owner Responsibilities

Each Risk Owner must:

- Understand the assigned risk.
- Evaluate its probability.
- Evaluate its potential impact.
- Maintain mitigation actions.
- Monitor changes in risk exposure.
- Track mitigation effectiveness.
- Report material changes to the appropriate Business Unit leadership.

Risk ownership cannot be delegated without explicitly assigning a new Risk Owner.

## 3.3 Mehkam Responsibilities

Mehkam is responsible for:

- Monitoring governance-level risks.
- Reviewing important risk reports.
- Maintaining portfolio-level risk visibility.
- Ensuring consistency in risk governance.
- Protecting Holding-level interests.
- Identifying risks that may affect multiple businesses.
- Monitoring risks that may materially affect the Mehkam ecosystem.

# 4. Risk Categories

Mehkam recognizes multiple categories of organizational risk.

The categories provide a common classification system but are not intended to limit the identification of other relevant risks.

## 4.1 Strategic Risks

Risks affecting the long-term direction, positioning, or objectives of a Business Unit.

Examples include:

- Incorrect market direction.
- Poor strategic decisions.
- Loss of competitive advantage.
- Failure to achieve strategic objectives.
- Incorrect business positioning.
- Major changes in market conditions.

## 4.2 Operational Risks

Risks affecting daily business execution.

Examples include:

- Process failures.
- Team execution problems.
- Operational inefficiencies.
- Resource limitations.
- Supplier or operational dependencies.
- Internal process weaknesses.

## 4.3 Financial Risks

Risks affecting financial stability and performance.

Examples include:

- Revenue instability.
- Unexpected cost increases.
- Incorrect resource allocation.
- Cash flow problems.
- Financial planning errors.
- Unsustainable cost structures.

## 4.4 Legal and Compliance Risks

Risks related to legal obligations, contracts, regulations, and compliance requirements.

Examples include:

- Contractual disputes.
- Compliance failures.
- Legal disputes.
- Regulatory changes.
- Intellectual property risks.
- Corporate legal exposure.

## 4.5 Technology Risks

Risks related to technology systems, infrastructure, security, and engineering capabilities.

Examples include:

- Security vulnerabilities.
- System failures.
- Infrastructure failures.
- Technical debt.
- Technology obsolescence.
- Architecture limitations.
- Dependency failures.

## 4.6 Organizational Risks

Risks related to people, organizational structure, leadership, and internal capabilities.

Examples include:

- Leadership gaps.
- Critical talent dependency.
- Organizational instability.
- Capability shortages.
- Poor internal communication.
- Insufficient succession planning.

# 5. Risk Assessment Approach

Mehkam uses a structured risk assessment approach based primarily on:

- Probability.
- Impact.

The basic risk scoring model is:

```text
Risk Score = Probability × Impact
```

The general assessment process is:

```text id="3fj8zi"
Risk Identification
        ↓
Probability Assessment
        ↓
Impact Assessment
        ↓
Risk Score Calculation
        ↓
Risk Prioritization
        ↓
Mitigation Plan
```

Risk assessment should consider both the current exposure and the potential future impact of the risk.

Business Units should periodically reassess risks when their probability, impact, or surrounding conditions materially change.

# 6. Risk Mitigation Principles

Business Units are responsible for creating and executing risk mitigation strategies.

Common mitigation approaches include:

- Preventive actions.
- Process improvements.
- Technical improvements.
- Resource adjustments.
- Operational changes.
- Redundancy and resilience measures.
- Monitoring and control mechanisms.

Business Units must:

- Create appropriate mitigation plans.
- Assign responsibilities.
- Define expected outcomes.
- Execute mitigation actions.
- Monitor effectiveness.
- Update mitigation plans when conditions change.

Risk mitigation should be proportional to the potential impact and probability of the risk.

# 7. Risk Monitoring and Reporting

Each Business Unit must continuously monitor identified risks.

Risk records and reports should include:

- Risk description.
- Risk category.
- Risk Owner.
- Probability.
- Impact.
- Risk Score.
- Current status.
- Mitigation strategy.
- Mitigation status.
- Remaining exposure.
- Future actions.

The general reporting model is:

```text id="l2g4xw"
Risk Monitoring
        ↓
Business Unit Risk Report
        ↓
Governance Review
        ↓
Mehkam Oversight
```

Reporting should provide sufficient visibility for governance without requiring Mehkam to manage operational risk directly.

# 8. Risk Escalation Model

Business Units manage their own risks within their approved authority.

Material risks that may affect the broader Mehkam ecosystem should be reported through the established governance reporting process.

Escalation to Mehkam may be appropriate when a risk:

- Has material portfolio-level impact.
- May affect multiple Business Units.
- Threatens ownership or strategic interests.
- Exceeds the Business Unit's authority.
- Requires holding-level resources.
- Creates significant legal or financial exposure.
- May materially affect Mehkam's long-term value.

The general escalation model is:

```text id="t3tq8s"
Business Unit Risk
        ↓
Risk Assessment
        ↓
Business Unit Mitigation
        ↓
Governance Reporting
        ↓
Mehkam Review
        ↓
Guidance / Strategic Action
```

Immediate escalation is not required for ordinary operational risks unless future governance policies define specific escalation thresholds.

# 9. Risk Governance Principles

## 9.1 Risk Ownership Stays Close to the Source

The Business Unit closest to the risk should own its identification, assessment, and mitigation.

## 9.2 Accountability Must Be Explicit

Every material risk must have a clearly assigned Risk Owner.

## 9.3 Prevention Is Preferable to Reaction

Organizations should identify and mitigate risks before they become operational incidents whenever reasonably possible.

## 9.4 Risk Management Must Support Execution

Risk processes should protect the organization without creating unnecessary operational bureaucracy.

## 9.5 Material Risks Must Remain Visible

Business Unit independence does not remove the requirement for appropriate governance visibility.

## 9.6 Long-Term Value Must Be Protected

Risk decisions should consider the long-term financial, strategic, technological, and organizational value of the Mehkam ecosystem.

# Scope

This framework applies to:

- Mehkam Holding.
- All Business Units.
- Subsidiaries.
- Business leadership teams.
- Engineering teams.
- Business teams.
- Strategic initiatives.
- Operational activities.
- Portfolio-level risks.

It applies to strategic, operational, financial, legal, compliance, technology, organizational, and other material risks affecting Mehkam-owned businesses.

The framework should be applied together with Mehkam's governance, strategy, decision-making, and investment frameworks.

# Governance

The Mehkam Risk Management Framework is an official organizational framework.

Business Units are responsible for maintaining their own risk management processes within the boundaries established by Mehkam governance.

Mehkam maintains governance oversight and receives appropriate risk visibility through periodic reporting.

The framework should be reviewed when:

- Mehkam's governance model changes.
- The Business Unit operating model materially changes.
- New material risk categories emerge.
- Portfolio structure changes significantly.
- Major risk-management requirements change.
- Scheduled governance reviews take place.

Any material modification to this framework must follow Mehkam's governance and documentation review process.

# Final Statement

The Mehkam Risk Management Framework establishes a decentralized approach in which Business Units own and manage their risks while Mehkam protects the long-term stability, strategic interests, and value of the ecosystem.

Through clear risk ownership, structured assessment, proportional mitigation, continuous monitoring, and appropriate governance visibility, Mehkam enables businesses to operate independently without compromising organizational resilience.

The objective of risk management is not to eliminate risk, but to ensure that risks are understood, owned, managed, and governed at the appropriate organizational level.
