# Visual Identity System

---

<div align="center">
  <img src="../../assets/logo/png/mehkam-symbol-white-2048w.png" alt="Mehkam Logo" width="150" />
</div>

## Overview

This document defines the official Visual Identity System of Mehkam Holding.

It establishes the visual language governing imagery, composition, visual hierarchy, aesthetic direction, experimentation, and prohibited visual approaches across the Mehkam ecosystem.

The purpose of this system is to ensure that every visual expression of Mehkam communicates the same fundamental characteristics:

- Authority
- Structure
- Luxury
- Restraint
- Professionalism
- Longevity
- Distinctiveness

The Visual Identity System is designed to provide strong visual consistency while allowing controlled evolution across the organization's communication touchpoints.

---

# 1. Visual Identity Principles

## 1.1 Core Visual Traits

The visual identity of Mehkam is defined by five primary characteristics:

1. **Subtle**
2. **Luxurious**
3. **Structured**
4. **Composed**
5. **Timeless**

These characteristics must remain recognizable across all visual touchpoints.

Visual execution should avoid unnecessary visual noise, excessive decoration, or short-lived stylistic decisions that may reduce the perceived authority of the organization.

---

## 1.2 Brand Formality

Mehkam maintains a fully formal and organizational visual tone.

Visual communication must reflect the character of a serious organization capable of leadership, strategic decision-making, and long-term execution.

The visual language must therefore communicate:

- Institutional confidence
- Strategic control
- Organizational maturity
- Leadership
- Stability
- Authority

---

## 1.3 Perception Objective

The first visual impression of Mehkam should communicate:

> **Power and authority through structure, discipline, and visual consistency.**

Power should not be communicated through aggressive visual effects, excessive contrast, decorative complexity, or promotional aesthetics.

Instead, authority should emerge from:

- Order
- Precision
- Scale
- Composition
- Restraint
- Consistency
- Controlled visual tension

---

# 2. Imagery Direction

## 2.1 Primary Imagery Direction

Mehkam imagery is primarily human-centered.

Primary subjects include:

- Leaders
- Executives
- Decision-makers
- Professional teams
- Leadership interactions
- Strategic discussions
- Organizational environments

Human presence should communicate leadership, responsibility, decision-making, and professional interaction.

---

## 2.2 Secondary Imagery

Although human subjects are the primary visual focus, Mehkam may use secondary imagery independently.

Secondary imagery includes:

- Luxury architecture
- Architectural details
- Premium materials
- Controlled environmental compositions
- Structural details
- High-quality interior spaces

These elements must remain consistent with the primary visual language.

They must not become decorative imagery without strategic or compositional purpose.

---

## 2.3 Human Representation

People must be represented with high realism.

Human imagery should demonstrate:

- Natural anatomy
- Natural facial expressions
- Natural body language
- Realistic interaction
- Professional behavior
- Controlled presence

Professional photographic correction is permitted.

However, exaggerated beauty treatment, facial modification, or artificial perfection is prohibited.

---

## 2.4 Leadership Representation

Leaders should primarily appear in situations associated with:

- Decision-making
- Direction
- Leadership
- Strategic interaction
- Organizational control
- Professional communication

Leadership should be communicated through context and positioning rather than exaggerated poses or theatrical behavior.

---

## 2.5 Posing and Movement

Human movement must remain:

- Natural
- Controlled
- Professional
- Contextually believable

Artificial advertising poses are prohibited.

Individuals must not appear to be deliberately posing for a generic corporate advertisement.

---

## 2.6 Framing

Preferred framing is:

- Close-up
- Portrait-oriented
- Character-focused

The composition should emphasize:

- Facial presence
- Personality
- Leadership
- Character
- Human expression

Wide environmental framing may be used when the architectural context is strategically important.

---

# 3. Environmental and Architectural Direction

## 3.1 Architectural Character

Architectural imagery should communicate:

- Luxury
- Structure
- Scale
- Depth
- Authority
- Permanence

The preferred environmental treatment is:

> **Dark and dramatic, with light and shadow functioning as primary compositional elements.**

---

## 3.2 Lighting Direction

Preferred lighting is:

- Dark
- Dramatic
- Controlled
- Directional
- High in depth

Lighting should create visual depth and authority without becoming artificial or promotional.

Lighting must feel physically believable.

Artificial advertising lighting is prohibited.

---

## 3.3 Material and Spatial Expression

Architectural and environmental imagery may include premium materials and high-quality spaces.

The visual treatment should prioritize:

- Texture
- Material quality
- Geometry
- Depth
- Shadow
- Architectural structure

Luxury must be communicated through quality and restraint rather than visual excess.

---

# 4. Color and Image Treatment

## 4.1 Color Direction

Mehkam imagery should primarily use:

> **Monochromatic or near-black-and-white treatment.**

Color usage must communicate:

- Depth
- Authority
- Restraint
- Luxury
- Seriousness

Bright, cheerful, highly saturated, or visually excessive colors are prohibited.

---

## 4.2 Post-Processing

Post-processing must remain minimal.

The objective is to preserve:

- Natural skin texture
- Realistic materials
- Natural lighting
- Authentic facial details
- Photographic realism

Heavy manipulation, excessive retouching, artificial color grading, or visually obvious processing must be avoided.

---

# 5. Realism Standard

## 5.1 Photographic Realism

Mehkam imagery must be highly realistic.

Images must appear as authentic professional photography rather than:

- CGI
- Illustration
- Artificial rendering
- Synthetic imagery
- Obviously AI-generated imagery

---

## 5.2 AI-Generated Imagery

AI-generated imagery may only be used when the final output meets the same visual standards as professional real-world photography.

An image must be rejected if it visibly demonstrates artificial generation through:

- Unrealistic anatomy
- Artificial facial details
- Incorrect hands
- Unnatural expressions
- Synthetic environments
- Unrealistic interaction
- Artificial lighting
- Visible generative artifacts

The production method does not determine acceptance.

The final visual result determines acceptance.

---

# 6. Composition Rules

## 6.1 Grid System

Mehkam compositions must be highly structured.

A defined Grid and precise Alignment system should be used to establish:

- Order
- Consistency
- Balance
- Hierarchy
- Visual control

Creative freedom must operate within the established structural system.

---

## 6.2 Alignment

Alignment must be precise and intentional.

Major visual elements should demonstrate clear relationships with:

- Grid boundaries
- Typography
- Logo placement
- Image boundaries
- Content blocks

Random or visually ambiguous alignment should be avoided.

---

## 6.3 Symmetry

Mehkam compositions should generally demonstrate a high degree of symmetry.

Symmetry should reinforce:

- Formality
- Stability
- Authority
- Control
- Institutional character

Asymmetry may be introduced only when it improves the intended composition without weakening the formal visual character.

---

## 6.4 Whitespace

Whitespace is important but controlled.

Whitespace should be used to:

- Separate visual elements
- Improve hierarchy
- Preserve readability
- Establish balance
- Create visual breathing room

Whitespace should not become an arbitrary decorative device.

---

## 6.5 Image and Text Relationship

Text may be placed directly over imagery when readability remains controlled.

There is no fixed global ratio between image and text.

The relationship should be determined according to:

- Communication objective
- Content hierarchy
- Medium
- Composition
- Context

---

## 6.6 Visual Hierarchy

There is no single mandatory hierarchy for every Mehkam composition.

The hierarchy should be determined by the objective of each composition.

Depending on the use case, priority may be given to:

- Message
- Image
- Headline
- Supporting content
- Brand identifier

However, hierarchy must always remain intentional and structurally clear.

---

## 6.7 Logo Placement

Logo placement must be:

- Structured
- Consistent
- Repeatable
- Grid-aligned

The Mehkam logo should use predefined placement zones rather than arbitrary positioning.

Logo placement must remain consistent across recurring asset types.

---

## 6.8 Decorative Elements

Decorative elements should be minimal.

Every visual element must have a functional or compositional purpose.

Elements that exist only to increase visual complexity should not be introduced.

---

## 6.9 Visual Density

Mehkam compositions may have high information density.

High density is acceptable when required by the communication objective.

However, density should not automatically be interpreted as visual disorder.

The following distinction must be maintained:

> **High Information Density ≠ Uncontrolled Visual Clutter**

Grid, Alignment, Hierarchy, and spacing must be used to control dense compositions.

---

## 6.10 Visual Rhythm

The default visual rhythm of Mehkam is:

> **Calm, stable, and controlled.**

Visual compositions should communicate:

- Stability
- Confidence
- Control
- Authority

Rapid visual movement, excessive variation, and high-energy compositions should not become default characteristics of the brand.

---

# 7. Typography in Visual Composition

Typography should communicate:

- Luxury
- Formality
- Authority
- Brand personality

Typography must remain consistent with the established Mehkam Typography System.

Excessively playful, casual, decorative, or informal typography is prohibited.

Typography should function as part of the structural composition rather than as an independent decorative element.

---

# 8. Visual Distinctiveness

## 8.1 Distinctiveness Objective

Mehkam should maintain a high level of visual distinctiveness.

The brand should be recognizable through its combined use of:

- Art Direction
- Imagery
- Tonality
- Composition
- Typography
- Structural consistency
- Visual restraint

Distinctiveness must not be achieved through visual noise or excessive stylistic experimentation.

---

## 8.2 Distinctiveness vs. Credibility

Visual distinctiveness is prioritized when it does not compromise brand credibility.

The following principle applies:

> **Distinctiveness may increase, but credibility must not decrease.**

Any visual direction that makes Mehkam appear:

- Cheap
- Artificial
- Promotional
- Immature
- Playful
- Unprofessional

must be rejected.

---

# 9. Trend and Evolution Policy

## 9.1 Trend Adoption

Visual trends may contribute to the evolution of Mehkam's visual identity.

However:

> **Trends may evolve the system without changing the Core Identity.**

Trend adoption must therefore remain selective and controlled.

---

## 9.2 Core Identity Protection

The following elements must remain stable:

- Core visual character
- Brand personality
- Authority
- Formality
- Luxury
- Structural discipline
- Visual credibility

Trends must not redefine these foundational characteristics.

---

## 9.3 Visual Language Flexibility

The Mehkam Visual Language is highly stable.

Changes should be:

- Limited
- Intentional
- Controlled
- Governed

The system should evolve gradually rather than through abrupt visual transformations.

---

# 10. Visual Experimentation

## 10.1 Experimentation Level

Mehkam allows a **moderate level of visual experimentation**.

Experimentation is permitted to discover:

- New visual directions
- New composition approaches
- New image treatments
- New communication patterns
- New creative techniques

---

## 10.2 Experimentation Scope

Experimentation may occur across all Mehkam Touchpoints.

However, all experimentation remains subject to Brand Governance.

No Touchpoint is exempt from the fundamental visual standards defined by this document.

---

## 10.3 Experimentation Boundaries

Experimentation must not compromise:

- Brand credibility
- Core visual identity
- Professionalism
- Visual consistency
- Premium perception
- Realism
- Structural discipline

---

# 11. Prohibited Visual Styles

The following visual approaches are explicitly prohibited within the Mehkam Visual Identity System.

## 11.1 Imagery Prohibitions

The following are prohibited:

- Stock photography
- Generic corporate photography
- Cliché corporate scenes
- Artificially posed people
- Unrealistic human interaction
- Unprofessional human behavior
- Artificial or promotional lighting
- Obviously AI-generated appearance
- Exaggerated beauty treatment
- Facial modification
- Artificial anatomy
- Synthetic-looking environments

---

## 11.2 Aesthetic Prohibitions

The following are prohibited:

- Extremely bright colors
- Cheerful or excessively colorful visual palettes
- Flashy visual treatments
- Fantasy aesthetics
- Cartoon aesthetics
- Playful visual language
- Excessive decorative complexity
- Uncontrolled visual clutter

---

## 11.3 Graphic Design Prohibitions

The following are prohibited unless explicitly defined as part of an approved future Brand System:

- Strong decorative gradients
- Dramatic visual effects
- Unnecessary shadows
- Unnecessary Glow effects
- Unnecessary 3D effects
- Decorative shapes without functional purpose
- Excessively playful typography
- Overly informal typography

---

## 11.4 Promotional Aesthetics

Luxury must not be expressed through conventional advertising aesthetics.

A visually luxurious composition must not become:

- Promotional
- Sales-oriented
- Commercially exaggerated
- Artificially glamorous

The following principle applies:

> **Luxury must communicate institutional quality, not commercial persuasion.**

---

# 12. Hard Rejection Criteria

A visual asset must be rejected when one or more of the following conditions are present:

1. It appears obviously artificial or AI-generated.
2. Human interaction appears unnatural.
3. Human posing appears artificially promotional.
4. The visual treatment compromises professional credibility.
5. Lighting appears artificial or commercially staged.
6. Beauty treatment significantly alters human appearance.
7. Facial identity is artificially modified.
8. The composition becomes decorative without purpose.
9. The visual language conflicts with Mehkam's Core Identity.
10. The asset communicates a promotional aesthetic inconsistent with Mehkam.

A visually attractive asset is not sufficient for approval.

**Brand compatibility is mandatory.**

---

# 13. Visual Governance

## 13.1 Governance Principle

All visual experimentation, evolution, and implementation must remain subject to Mehkam Brand Governance.

No individual Touchpoint may independently redefine the visual identity.

---

## 13.2 Touchpoint Consistency

The Visual Identity System applies across all relevant Mehkam Touchpoints, including:

- Website
- Social Media
- Presentations
- Corporate Documents
- Marketing Materials
- Digital Products
- Corporate Communications
- Campaign Assets
- Internal Communications
- Future Brand Touchpoints

The execution may adapt to the requirements of each medium.

The Core Visual Language must remain consistent.

---

## 13.3 Exception Policy

Visual elements that are fundamentally incompatible with the Mehkam Visual Identity are prohibited even when they may appear effective for a specific Touchpoint or campaign.

There are no exceptions for:

- Campaign convenience
- Short-term trends
- Promotional performance
- Individual creative preference

Brand integrity takes precedence.

---

# 14. Core Visual Standard

The Mehkam Visual Identity System can be summarized through the following principles:

> **Structured.**
>
> **Luxurious.**
>
> **Restrained.**
>
> **Real.**
>
> **Authoritative.**
>
> **Distinctive.**
>
> **Timeless.**

Mehkam should look powerful because it is controlled, not because it is excessive.

Mehkam should look luxurious because it is refined, not because it is flashy.

Mehkam should look distinctive because it is intentional, not because it follows temporary trends.

Mehkam should look human because it is authentic, not because it uses generic corporate imagery.

---

# 15. Implementation Principle

Every future Mehkam visual asset should be evaluated against three fundamental questions:

### 1. Does it look like Mehkam?

The asset must demonstrate recognizable alignment with the Core Visual Identity.

### 2. Does it strengthen Mehkam?

The asset must reinforce authority, credibility, structure, luxury, or strategic character.

### 3. Does it remain consistent with the system?

The asset must operate within the defined Imagery, Composition, Typography, and Governance standards.

If the answer to any of these questions is **No**, the asset requires revision or rejection.

# Final Statement

This document establishes the official Visual Identity System of Mehkam Holding.

It defines the visual principles, imagery direction, composition rules, experimentation boundaries, distinctiveness requirements, and prohibited visual approaches that govern the visual expression of Mehkam.

The system is designed to preserve a stable and recognizable Core Identity while allowing controlled evolution through disciplined experimentation and Brand Governance.

The fundamental objective is to ensure that every Mehkam visual expression communicates:

> **Authority through structure.**
>
> **Luxury through restraint.**
>
> **Distinctiveness through intentionality.**
>
> **Credibility through realism.**
>
> **Longevity through consistency.**
