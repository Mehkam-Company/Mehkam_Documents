# Master Typography System

---

<div align="center">
  <img src="../../assets/logo/png/mehkam-symbol-white-2048w.png" alt="Mehkam Logo" width="150" />
</div>

## Overview

This document defines the authoritative typography system of Mehkam Holding.

It establishes the official typeface, weight allocation, typographic hierarchy, usage principles, and implementation rules across Mehkam brand touchpoints.

The system is designed to communicate:

- Authority
- Luxury
- Formality
- Restraint
- Precision
- Stability
- Professionalism

Typography is treated as a structural component of the Mehkam Brand System rather than a decorative element.

---

# 1. Typography Philosophy

The Mehkam typography system follows a controlled and structured approach.

Its character is:

> **Formal, luxurious, restrained, and authoritative.**

Typography must reinforce the organizational character of Mehkam without becoming visually excessive, playful, or overly expressive.

The system prioritizes:

1. Hierarchy
2. Readability
3. Consistency
4. Authority
5. Visual restraint
6. Brand recognition

---

# 2. Primary Typeface

## 2.1 Peyda

**Primary Typeface:** Peyda

Peyda is the official primary typeface of Mehkam Holding.

It is used across the brand system for:

- Corporate communication
- Brand documentation
- Digital interfaces
- Websites
- Presentations
- Marketing materials
- Social media
- Product interfaces
- Print materials

The same primary typeface should be maintained across digital and print environments whenever technically possible.

---

# 3. Typeface Role

Peyda serves as the primary typographic voice of Mehkam.

Its usage must maintain a consistent visual character across all touchpoints.

The typeface should communicate:

- Corporate authority
- Modern professionalism
- Controlled luxury
- Structural clarity
- Editorial confidence

Typography must never undermine the formal and premium positioning of the brand.

---

# 4. Weight System

Typography weights are assigned according to **content importance and semantic hierarchy**.

Weight selection must not be arbitrary.

The general principle is:

> **Higher importance → stronger typographic weight.**

However, weight must always remain subordinate to hierarchy and readability.

---

## 4.1 Weight Roles

The available Peyda weights should be mapped to functional roles rather than used freely.

| Weight Role | Recommended Usage                                             |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Thin        | Exceptional editorial or large-scale display usage            |
| ExtraLight  | Large display compositions where visual lightness is required |
| Light       | Secondary display or restrained editorial content             |
| Regular     | Primary body text                                             |
| Medium      | Supporting text and emphasized body content                   |
| SemiBold    | Subheadings and important labels                              |
| Bold        | Major headings and high-priority content                      |
| ExtraBold   | Display-level emphasis and major brand statements             |
| Black       | Exceptional display emphasis                                  |

Not every weight is required in every touchpoint.

---

# 5. Weight Allocation Principle

Weight selection must follow content hierarchy.

A practical hierarchy is:

```text id="d9c8km"
Highest Importance
        ↓
Black / ExtraBold
        ↓
Bold
        ↓
SemiBold
        ↓
Medium
        ↓
Regular
        ↓
Light / ExtraLight
        ↓
Lowest Visual Weight
```

The actual weight used should depend on:

- Content importance
- Display size
- Background
- Density
- Medium
- Reading distance
- Intended emphasis

Weight should never be used as a substitute for proper hierarchy.

---

# 6. Typography Hierarchy

The Mehkam typography hierarchy consists of five primary levels:

```text id="4oh6vp"
Display
   ↓
Heading
   ↓
Body
   ↓
Caption
   ↓
Label
```

Each level has a distinct functional purpose.

---

# 7. Display

Display typography is reserved for major visual statements.

Typical usage:

- Brand statements
- Campaign headlines
- Major presentation titles
- Hero sections
- Executive communication
- Large editorial compositions

Display typography may use stronger weights and larger sizes.

The visual treatment must remain controlled and premium.

Recommended weight range:

**Bold → ExtraBold → Black**

Display typography should not become decorative typography.

---

# 8. Heading

Heading typography establishes structural hierarchy.

Typical usage:

- Section headings
- Page titles
- Document sections
- Product headings
- Presentation sections
- Marketing content

Recommended weight range:

**SemiBold → Bold**

Heading levels should be visually distinguishable without creating excessive contrast.

---

# 9. Body

Body typography is optimized for reading and information delivery.

Typical usage:

- Paragraphs
- Documentation
- Product content
- Corporate communication
- Long-form content
- Descriptions

Recommended primary weight:

**Regular**

Medium may be used for controlled emphasis.

Body typography must prioritize:

- Readability
- Line length
- Line height
- Consistent rhythm
- Appropriate contrast

---

# 10. Caption

Caption typography provides supporting information.

Typical usage:

- Metadata
- Image descriptions
- Secondary information
- Footnotes
- Supporting explanations
- References

Recommended weight range:

**Regular → Medium**

Caption text must remain visually subordinate to Body content.

---

# 11. Label

Labels identify controls, categories, statuses, and compact pieces of information.

Typical usage:

- UI labels
- Navigation
- Tags
- Status indicators
- Metadata
- Form labels

Recommended weight range:

**Medium → SemiBold**

Labels should remain concise and structurally clear.

---

# 12. Typographic Hierarchy Rules

Hierarchy must be created through a controlled combination of:

- Size
- Weight
- Line height
- Spacing
- Position
- Contrast

Typography hierarchy must not rely exclusively on font size.

The system should maintain a clear relationship between:

```text id="y5y6lh"
Importance
    ↓
Scale
    ↓
Weight
    ↓
Spacing
    ↓
Position
```

---

# 13. Size System

Exact type sizes may vary by platform and medium.

However, the relative hierarchy must remain consistent.

Recommended conceptual scale:

| Level   | Relative Scale | Primary Role              |
| ------- | -------------- | ------------------------- |
| Display | Very Large     | Major visual statement    |
| Heading | Large          | Structural hierarchy      |
| Body    | Base           | Reading                   |
| Caption | Small          | Supporting information    |
| Label   | Compact        | Functional identification |

Digital products may define exact pixel/rem values within their Design System while preserving this hierarchy.

Print systems may define exact point sizes according to the physical format and reading distance.

---

# 14. Line Height

Line height must be determined according to:

- Typeface size
- Content density
- Reading environment
- Language
- Medium

Body content should maintain comfortable reading rhythm.

Display and Heading typography may use tighter line height when appropriate.

Overly compressed line height that compromises readability is prohibited.

---

# 15. Letter Spacing

Letter spacing must remain controlled.

The general principle is:

- Large display text may use slightly adjusted tracking.
- Headings should use restrained tracking.
- Body text should prioritize natural readability.
- Small labels may require controlled spacing depending on size and medium.

Excessive letter spacing must not be used to create an artificial luxury aesthetic.

---

# 16. Digital Usage

Peyda is the primary typeface for Mehkam digital products and platforms.

Digital implementation should prioritize:

- Screen readability
- Consistent hierarchy
- Responsive scaling
- Accessibility
- Rendering consistency
- Performance

The typography system should be implemented through centralized Design Tokens wherever possible.

Example:

```text id="z8sv3j"
typography
├── display
├── heading
├── body
├── caption
└── label
```

Each semantic role should reference controlled typography values rather than arbitrary component-level definitions.

---

# 17. Print Usage

The same typography system applies to print materials.

Peyda should be used for:

- Corporate documents
- Presentations
- Reports
- Marketing materials
- Executive communication
- Brand collateral

Print implementations may adjust:

- Point size
- Line height
- Tracking
- Weight

according to:

- Physical size
- Paper
- Printing process
- Reading distance
- Production requirements

The underlying typographic hierarchy must remain consistent.

---

# 18. Digital and Print Consistency

Mehkam uses a unified typography system across Digital and Print.

The same hierarchy must communicate the same semantic meaning regardless of medium.

For example:

```text
Display → Major Statement
Heading → Structure
Body → Information
Caption → Supporting Information
Label → Identification
```

The visual implementation may adapt to the medium, but the semantic hierarchy must remain consistent.

---

# 19. Typography Density

Typography should support Mehkam's structured visual language.

Dense information may be used where the application requires it, but density must remain controlled through:

- Grid
- Spacing
- Hierarchy
- Line length
- Weight
- Grouping

Typography must not create visual chaos.

---

# 20. Typography and Brand Character

Typography must reinforce Mehkam's core visual traits:

- Refined
- Luxurious
- Structured
- Composed
- Enduring

It must communicate authority through structure rather than decorative complexity.

The preferred expression is:

> **Quiet authority.**

---

# 21. Prohibited Typography

The following are prohibited:

- Playful typography
- Cartoon-like type treatments
- Excessively decorative fonts
- Unnecessary font mixing
- Excessive weight variation
- Excessive tracking
- Decorative text effects
- Artificial 3D typography
- Glow effects
- Excessive shadows
- Uncontrolled outlines
- Trend-driven typography that weakens brand consistency
- Informal typography inconsistent with corporate positioning

---

# 22. Typeface Mixing

Peyda should remain the default and dominant typeface across the Mehkam ecosystem.

Introducing additional typefaces requires a clear functional or technical justification.

A secondary typeface must never be introduced solely for visual novelty.

Any permanent addition to the official typography system requires Brand Governance approval.

---

# 23. Typography Accessibility

Typography must remain accessible across digital and physical environments.

Implementation should consider:

- Sufficient contrast
- Appropriate text size
- Comfortable line height
- Readable line length
- Clear hierarchy
- Responsive behavior

Brand expression must never justify sacrificing basic readability.

---

# 24. Governance

This document is the authoritative source for the Mehkam Master Typography System.

Any change to:

- Primary Typeface
- Typeface family
- Weight system
- Hierarchy
- Typography roles
- Official typography tokens

must follow Mehkam Brand Governance.

Individual teams, products, campaigns, or designers may adapt typography to their medium but may not redefine the underlying brand system independently.

---

# 25. Core Typography Rules

The following rules are mandatory:

1. **Peyda is the primary Mehkam typeface.**
2. **Typography must remain formal, luxurious, restrained, and authoritative.**
3. **Weight is assigned according to content importance.**
4. **The hierarchy consists of Display, Heading, Body, Caption, and Label.**
5. **Digital and print use the same underlying typography system.**
6. **Typography must prioritize readability and structural clarity.**
7. **Additional typefaces require governance approval.**
8. **Decorative and playful typography is prohibited.**
9. **Typography effects must remain minimal and purposeful.**
10. **Typography must reinforce the broader Mehkam Visual Identity System.**

---

# 26. Final Typography Principle

The Mehkam Typography System follows one fundamental principle:

> **Typography should communicate authority through hierarchy, not decoration.**

Peyda provides the typographic foundation.

Weight establishes importance.

Hierarchy establishes structure.

Spacing establishes rhythm.

Consistency establishes recognition.

Together, these elements create a formal, luxurious, and enduring typographic language for Mehkam Holding.
